A catalog of books mentioned on Marginal Revolution

Marginalia Library is an independent catalog of books mentioned on Marginal Revolution, the blog Tyler Cowen and Alex Tabarrok have written since 2003. It files 7,952 works and 13,286 mentions of them, from 7,289 posts. Each work has a card listing every mention: its date, who wrote the post, MR’s evaluation of the book, and the post’s title, linked to the post.

How to use it

Look it up
Search for a title or an author in the box at the top of every page. A small typo is fine.
Open a drawer
The catalog is shelved by subject, form, place and period. Each drawer holds smaller drawers, down to a shelf of works.
Search with filters
Narrow the whole catalog at once: by subject, place and period, by who mentioned a book, by MR’s evaluation, or by when it was published or mentioned. Every filtered list has its own address, to bookmark or share.
Read a card
A work’s card shows its shelving, MR’s overall evaluation, a timeline with one mark per mention, every mention with a link to its post, and related works.
Follow an author or a year
Authors can be sorted by mentions, recommendations or evaluations. Every year and month has a page of the posts that mention books.
See the patterns
Twelve patterns on one page, each with its charts: the most-mentioned books and authors, what MR reads, Tyler and Alex compared, where MR’s evaluations disagree, and books that went from the pile to read.
Surprise me
A random book’s card. Narrow it down by form, subject, place, period, writer or evaluation, or start from “a novel Tyler liked”. The card on this page is a quicker version.

How it was made

More on the method

Language models did the reading, and a person checked their work. OpenAI’s GPT-6 Luna read all of the blog’s 40,000-odd posts and listed the books each one mentions. Each mention was then linked to a work in Open Library or Wikidata, with Anthropic’s Claude models choosing among candidates where the match wasn’t clear. Claude Sonnet 5.5 labeled what each post says about the book: its view, whether it recommends it, and whether the writer has read it. About 2,000 links and 650 labels were checked by hand. Some labels are surely still wrong.
